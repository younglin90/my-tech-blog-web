A smooth ball ought to slip through the air with less drag. A golf ball breaks that guess. Hundreds of dimples (the small dents on the ball) and it still flies nearly twice as far. The gap is the drag crisis: a stretch of Reynolds number where the drag coefficient $C_D$ falls off a cliff.

## Pressure Drag and Friction Drag

A ball feels two forces. Skin friction, from viscosity grazing the surface. Form drag, from the front-to-back pressure gap.

On a blunt body the pressure term is almost the whole load. The nose sits at high pressure where air hits it. The base stays at low pressure where the flow has left. That gap pulls the ball backward.

One coefficient carries the lot.

$$F_D = \tfrac{1}{2}\, \rho\, U^2\, A\, C_D$$

$\rho$ is density, $U$ relative speed, $A$ frontal area, $C_D$ the drag coefficient. What the golf ball changes is $C_D$. The value sits flat across Reynolds number, then drops.

$$Re = \frac{\rho\, U\, D}{\mu}$$

$D$ is diameter, $\mu$ viscosity. $Re$ is the ratio of inertia to viscous force.

## The Moment the Boundary Layer Leaves the Wall

Form drag is set by when the flow leaves the wall. That departure is boundary-layer separation.

Over the front half the air speeds up and pressure falls. Over the back half it reverses. The air slows and pressure climbs. An adverse pressure gradient: pressure rising in the flow direction.

Air near the wall has already spent most of its momentum on viscosity. It cannot push through that headwind. It stops, then gets shoved backward. The layer leaves there, and a wide low-pressure wake trails behind.

Earlier separation, wider wake. Wider wake, more form drag.

## The Division of Labor Between Laminar and Turbulent Layers

The separation angle depends on whether the layer is laminar or turbulent.

A laminar layer does not mix across itself. Near-wall air is slow and momentum-poor, so the adverse gradient wins early. About 82° from the front stagnation point.

A turbulent layer pulls fast outer air down to the wall. The near-wall fluid is sturdier and holds the same headwind longer. Separation waits until about 120°, and the wake narrows. The width change on the button above is this angle change. Why the wall region holds up at all is the same layered structure as [a pipe flow coming apart](/en/posts/2026-05-05-reynolds-pipe-transition-turbulence/).

## The Drag Crisis — $C_D$ Falls Off a Cliff

A smooth sphere holds $C_D$ near 0.4–0.5 from $Re \sim 10^3$ out to $3{\times}10^5$.

Near $Re \approx 3{\times}10^5$ it drops below 0.1. The flow has not reversed. The layer has transitioned from laminar to turbulent, and separation has moved aft. The narrower wake drags the coefficient off the cliff. That drop is the drag crisis.

Rougher surfaces trip the layer at a lower $Re$. Dimples are roughness put there on purpose. A golf ball's flight $Re$ (about $2{\times}10^5$) would still leave a smooth ball before the crisis, on the high-$C_D$ side. Dimples pull the cliff forward so the flight speed already sits on the low value. The curve you can drag by hand comes right after the printed numbers.

## Python — A Golf Ball's Drag Across the Crisis

Same speed for a smooth ball and a dimpled one. Diameter 0.0427 m, speed 70 m/s.

```python
import math

def cd_subcritical(Re):
    # Clift-Gauvin correlation (valid for Re < 3e5)
    return (24.0 / Re) * (1 + 0.15 * Re**0.687) + 0.42 / (1 + 42500 * Re**-1.16)

def smoothstep(x, a, b):
    t = max(0.0, min(1.0, (x - a) / (b - a)))
    return t * t * (3 - 2 * t)

def drag_coefficient(Re, roughness):
    # roughness 0 (smooth) to 1 (dimpled): shifts the critical Re leftward
    sub = cd_subcritical(Re)
    log_re = math.log10(Re)
    log_crit = 5.55 - 1.35 * roughness
    t = smoothstep(log_re, log_crit - 0.18, log_crit + 0.18)
    sup = 0.08 + 0.13 * smoothstep(log_re, log_crit, 7.0)  # post-crisis branch
    return sub * (1 - t) + sup * t

def drag_force(U, D, roughness, rho=1.2, mu=1.8e-5):
    Re = rho * U * D / mu
    Cd = drag_coefficient(Re, roughness)
    A = math.pi * (D / 2) ** 2          # frontal projected area
    Fd = 0.5 * rho * U**2 * A * Cd
    return Re, Cd, Fd

D = 0.0427   # golf ball diameter [m]
U = 70.0     # speed just off the driver [m/s]

for label, rough in [("smooth", 0.0), ("dimpled", 0.85)]:
    Re, Cd, Fd = drag_force(U, D, rough)
    print(f"{label}: Re={Re:.2e}  C_D={Cd:.3f}  F_D={Fd:.3f} N")
```

Output:

```text
smooth: Re=1.99e+05  C_D=0.487  F_D=2.049 N
dimpled: Re=1.99e+05  C_D=0.116  F_D=0.488 N
```

Raise the roughness slider and the cliff slides left. Park the logRe marker near 5.3 and the smooth curve is still on the plateau while the rough curve is already on the floor. Those two seats are the printed 0.487 and 0.116.

The printed 2.049 N and 0.488 N share one dynamic pressure. $0.5 \times 1.2 \times 70^{2} = 2940$ Pa times a frontal area near $1.432 \times 10^{-3}$ m² is 4.210 N. Times 0.487 that is 2.049 N. Times 0.116 it is 0.488 N. Ratio 4.20.

Critical log is $5.55 - 1.35 \times$ roughness: 5.55 when smooth ($Re \approx 3.55 \times 10^{5}$) and 4.40 at roughness 0.85 ($Re \approx 2.53 \times 10^{4}$). Flight log is 5.299. The smooth window opens at 5.37, so 5.299 stays outside and the subcritical branch returns 0.487. Dimples already sit on the supercritical branch, which runs from 0.08 toward 0.21 at log 7. At roughness 0.85 the smoothstep is 0.275, and $0.08 + 0.13 \times 0.275 = 0.116$.

Roughness 1 at that same 5.299 gives smoothstep 0.341, so $C_D$ is 0.124 and the force climbs to about 0.52 N. The dimple cliff's middle, $Re \approx 2.53 \times 10^{4}$, is 8.9 m/s. Slow the ball to that speed and the rough surface walks back toward a high $C_D$.

## Dimples, and Other Stages

The same angle shows up on other hardware. A car tail is shaped to delay separation and shrink the wake. A cricket ball is polished on one side so the two separation points do not match. A trip wire (a thin wire that forces the boundary layer turbulent) wrapped on a cylinder brings the crisis forward.

Drag is set less by the friction viscosity writes directly than by when the flow leaves the wall.

## Where Extra Roughness Puts the Drag Back

70 m/s is already past the crisis. Push roughness past 0.85 there and $C_D$ climbs from 0.116 back to 0.124. The printed factor of 4.20 is not the end of the slider.

Put dimples on a surface that also has to pass heat and the wall friction rises with the trip.

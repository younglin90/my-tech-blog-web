# How to Handle the Interface

One of the most fundamental questions in compressible multiphase flow simulation is
"how to numerically represent the **interface** between two fluids."

There are three main categories of methods:

1. **Volume of Fluid (VOF)**
2. **Level Set**
3. **Diffuse Interface**

## 1. Volume of Fluid (VOF)

### Principles

VOF tracks the **volume fraction** $\alpha$ occupied by a specific fluid in each cell.

$$
\frac{\partial \alpha}{\partial t} + \mathbf{u} \cdot \nabla \alpha = 0
$$

If $\alpha = 1$, the cell is full of fluid A; if $\alpha = 0$, it's full of fluid B; and if $0 < \alpha < 1$, the interface passes through the cell.

### Interface Reconstruction

The core of VOF is reconstructing the interface position within the cell from the $\alpha$ values:

- **SLIC** (Simple Line Interface Calculation): Approximates the interface parallel to the coordinate axes.
- **PLIC** (Piecewise Linear Interface Calculation): Approximates the interface as a line with an arbitrary slope.

In PLIC, the interface normal vector $\mathbf{n}$ is estimated from the gradient of the $\alpha$ field:

$$
\mathbf{n} = \frac{\nabla \alpha}{|\nabla \alpha|}
$$

### Pros and Cons

- **Pros**: Exact mass conservation (conservative form).
- **Cons**: Interface reconstruction is complex and computationally expensive. Difficult to extend to 3D. Inaccurate curvature calculation.

## 2. Level Set Method

### Principles

The interface is defined as the zero-level contour of a **signed distance function** $\phi$.

$$
\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial t} + \mathbf{u} \cdot \nabla \phi = 0
$$

- $\phi > 0$: Fluid A
- $\phi < 0$: Fluid B
- $\phi = 0$: Interface

### Re-initialization

During the advection process, $\phi$ loses its property as a signed distance function ($|\nabla\phi| = 1$).
To restore this, a **re-initialization** equation is solved:

$$
\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial \tau} + \text{sign}(\phi_0)(|\nabla\phi| - 1) = 0
$$

where $\tau$ is pseudo-time.

### Curvature and Surface Tension

A major advantage of the Level Set method is that geometric quantities are naturally calculated:

$$
\kappa = -\nabla \cdot \left(\frac{\nabla\phi}{|\nabla\phi|}\right)
$$

Surface tension is converted into a body force using the CSF (Continuum Surface Force) model:

$$
\mathbf{f}_s = \sigma \kappa \delta(\phi) \nabla\phi
$$

### Pros and Cons

- **Pros**: Easy calculation of curvature/normals. Handles topology changes naturally.
- **Cons**: **Fails mass conservation**. The interface position can shift during the re-initialization process.

### CLSVOF: Combining the Two

The **Coupled Level Set and VOF (CLSVOF)** method, which combines the mass conservation of VOF with the geometric advantages of Level Set, is widely used.

## 3. Diffuse Interface Method

### Shift in Philosophy

While the previous two methods view the interface as **sharp**, the diffuse interface method treats it as having an inherently **finite thickness**.

In this approach, no separate interface tracking equation is required, and the **volume fraction is included as part of the system of conservation laws**.

### 5-equation Model (Allaire et al., 2002; Kapila et al., 2001)

The most widely used reduced model:

$$
\frac{\partial (\alpha_1 \rho_1)}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\alpha_1 \rho_1 \mathbf{u}) = 0
$$

$$
\frac{\partial (\alpha_2 \rho_2)}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\alpha_2 \rho_2 \mathbf{u}) = 0
$$

$$
\frac{\partial (\rho \mathbf{u})}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\rho \mathbf{u} \otimes \mathbf{u} + p\mathbf{I}) = 0
$$

$$
\frac{\partial E}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot ((E + p)\mathbf{u}) = 0
$$

$$
\frac{\partial \alpha_1}{\partial t} + \mathbf{u} \cdot \nabla \alpha_1 = 0
$$

The last equation is key. Note that the advection equation for $\alpha_1$ is **non-conservative**.
This is the secret to **preventing pressure oscillations** at the interface.

### Why is Non-conservative Form Needed?

If $\alpha_1$ is solved in conservative form:

$$
\frac{\partial \alpha_1}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\alpha_1 \mathbf{u}) = \alpha_1 \nabla \cdot \mathbf{u}
$$

If the $\alpha_1 \nabla \cdot \mathbf{u}$ term on the right-hand side is not handled properly during discretization, unphysical pressure oscillations caused by EOS mixing occur.

Non-conservative advection maintains $\alpha_1$ as sharp at the interface while satisfying pressure/velocity equilibrium.

### 7-equation Model (Baer-Nunziato type)

A fully non-equilibrium model where each phase can have **independent pressure and velocity**:

$$
\frac{\partial \alpha_k \rho_k}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\alpha_k \rho_k \mathbf{u}_k) = 0, \quad k = 1, 2
$$

Convergence to pressure/velocity equilibrium is achieved through a relaxation process.
It is mathematically the most complete but has high computational costs.

## Comparison Summary

| Method | Mass Conservation | Interface Sharpness | Topology Change | Implementation Complexity | Suitability for Compressibility |
|------|:---------:|:----------:|:---------:|:----------:|:------------:|
| VOF (PLIC) | O | High | Difficult | High | Moderate |
| Level Set | X | High | Natural | Moderate | Moderate |
| CLSVOF | O | High | Natural | Very High | Moderate |
| 5-eq Diffuse | O | Moderate | Natural | Low | **Excellent** |
| 7-eq Diffuse | O | Moderate | Natural | High | **Excellent** |

## Conclusion

In compressible multiphase flows, the **Diffuse Interface method (especially the 5-equation model)** is the most natural and robust choice.
It shows outstanding performance in problems such as shock-interface interaction, cavitation, and underwater explosions.

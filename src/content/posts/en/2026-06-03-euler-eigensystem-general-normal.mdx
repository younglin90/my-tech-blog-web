I opened the laptop in a hotel room on a business trip. For three days my solver had been spitting negative densities, but only after I started rotating the grid. With $n_x=1, n_y=0$ everything ran clean. The moment $n_x=0.01$ showed up, the run blew up. Walking the log backwards landed me on a single line with a flipped sign — the last row of the left eigenvector matrix. That row was hiding something, and it took three days to find out what.

This post is the writeup. On an arbitrary unit normal $\hat n = (n_x, n_y, n_z)$, the 3D Euler eigenvalues and left/right eigenvectors fit on one page. We will see why one convention for the left eigenvectors always fails, and then build a Roe-type flux that runs in any direction.

## Conservative Euler, written along a normal

The 3D inviscid Euler equations are a vector conservation law:

$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{\mathrm{CV}} \mathbf{Q}\, dV + \oint_{\mathrm{CS}} \mathbf{F}\cdot \hat n\, dA = 0
$$

with $\mathbf{Q} = (\rho,\rho u,\rho v,\rho w,\rho e_o)^T$ the conservative state and $\mathbf{F}\cdot \hat n$ the normal flux on each face. The natural variable on a cell face is the normal velocity $v_n = u n_x + v n_y + w n_z$. The sound speed is $a^2 = \gamma p/\rho$, and the unit-normal constraint reads $n_x^2 + n_y^2 + n_z^2 = 1$.

The advantage of this coordinate choice is plain: every wave family lines up with the single variable $v_n$.

## The Jacobian A(n̂) — five waves inside a 5×5

Differentiating $\mathbf{F}\cdot \hat n$ with respect to $\mathbf{Q}$ gives a Jacobian $A(\hat n)= \partial(\mathbf F\cdot\hat n)/\partial\mathbf Q$. We do not need a separate global $(x,y,z)$ matrix and a separate local $(\xi,\eta,\zeta)$ one — a single matrix carries $\hat n$ explicitly. This is the convention that has been the default since Yee–Kutler in 1983.

The characteristic polynomial $\det(A - \lambda I) = 0$ has five roots:

$$
\lambda_i = \{\, v_n - a,\; v_n,\; v_n + a,\; v_n,\; v_n\,\}
$$

The flow speed $v_n$ appears *three* times — twice as a repeated root. That repetition is the first clue for what later wrecks the left eigenvectors.

## Three families, two repeats — acoustic and entropy waves

The five eigenvalues come in *three families*. The flow speed $v_n$ itself is the **entropy wave**, and it carries two distinct signals at the same speed: entropy and tangential momentum. Hence the double repetition.

The pair $v_n \pm a$ are the **acoustic waves**. These two carry the pressure signal and the normal-velocity signal. In a Riemann fan, the middle ray is the contact, the outer ones are shocks or expansions.

## Right eigenvectors — written once

Solving $A R_i = \lambda_i R_i$ gives a 5×5 matrix. One convention (R-1) reads

$$
R =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 1 & 1 & 0 & 0 \\
u - a n_x & u & u + a n_x & n_y & -n_z \\
v - a n_y & v & v + a n_y & -n_x & 0 \\
w - a n_z & w & w + a n_z & 0 & n_x \\
h_o - a v_n & e_k & h_o + a v_n & u n_y - v n_x & w n_x - u n_z
\end{pmatrix}
$$

with $e_k = \tfrac12(u^2+v^2+w^2)$ the kinetic energy per unit mass and $h_o = h + e_k$ the total enthalpy per unit mass. The first three columns are paired with $\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_3$. The last two columns belong to the repeated $\lambda_4=\lambda_5=v_n$; they are two tangent-direction vectors, and which two depends on convention.

The third and fifth columns mark "which pair of tangent directions did you pick". R-2 and R-3 simply span the same subspace with a different basis pair.

## Left eigenvectors — the line that breaks at n_x → 0

Compute $L = R^{-1}$ and the last two rows develop $1/n_x$ entries. In the R-1 convention there is always a row that divides by $n_x$. A face with $\hat n = (0, 1, 0)$ — that is, $n_x = 0$ — sends those entries to infinity. That was the bug I hunted for three days.

The fix is small. R-2 divides by $n_y$; R-3 divides by $n_z$. Pick the convention per face by choosing the *largest component* of $|n_x|, |n_y|, |n_z|$ as the denominator. One line of code:

```python
def pick_convention(n):
    # n: ndarray of shape (3,), unit normal
    k = int(np.argmax(np.abs(n)))  # 0, 1, or 2
    return k  # use R-1, R-2, or R-3 accordingly
```

On every face one of the three conventions is safe. Without this branching, no unstructured-grid Euler code survives long.

## In 2D the singularity quietly vanishes

Drop $w = n_z = 0$ and the 5×5 becomes 4×4. The eigenvalues collapse to $\{v_n - a, v_n, v_n + a, v_n\}$, and the apparent singularities in the last row of $L$ cancel by hand:

$$
\frac{v - v_n n_y}{n_x} = \frac{v(1 - n_y^2) - u n_x n_y}{n_x} = v n_x - u n_y
$$

The $n_x$ in the denominator divides the numerator exactly. Anyone whose career stayed in 2D will never have met this trap. It appears only when you move to 3D.

## Python — a normal-direction Roe-type flux in one function

We collect the family eigenvalues and the 1D Roe-average wave strengths into a single function that returns the Roe-type flux on any face. The toy problem is a *tilted shock tube*: throw left/right states across the face $\hat n = (\cos 30°, \sin 30°, 0)$.

```python
import numpy as np

GAMMA = 1.4

def primitive_to_state(rho, vel, p):
    """rho: scalar, vel: (3,), p: scalar -> conservative Q (5,)"""
    rE = p / (GAMMA - 1) + 0.5 * rho * np.dot(vel, vel)
    return np.array([rho, rho * vel[0], rho * vel[1], rho * vel[2], rE])

def flux_normal(Q, n_hat):
    rho = Q[0]
    vel = Q[1:4] / rho
    rE = Q[4]
    p = (GAMMA - 1) * (rE - 0.5 * rho * np.dot(vel, vel))
    vn = np.dot(vel, n_hat)
    H = (rE + p) / rho
    return np.array([
        rho * vn,
        rho * vel[0] * vn + p * n_hat[0],
        rho * vel[1] * vn + p * n_hat[1],
        rho * vel[2] * vn + p * n_hat[2],
        rho * H * vn,
    ])

def roe_normal_flux(QL, QR, n_hat):
    rL, rR = QL[0], QR[0]
    vL = QL[1:4] / rL
    vR = QR[1:4] / rR
    pL = (GAMMA - 1) * (QL[4] - 0.5 * rL * np.dot(vL, vL))
    pR = (GAMMA - 1) * (QR[4] - 0.5 * rR * np.dot(vR, vR))
    HL = (QL[4] + pL) / rL
    HR = (QR[4] + pR) / rR

    sL, sR = np.sqrt(rL), np.sqrt(rR)
    inv = 1.0 / (sL + sR)
    rho = sL * sR
    vel = (sL * vL + sR * vR) * inv
    H   = (sL * HL + sR * HR) * inv
    vn  = np.dot(vel, n_hat)
    a2  = (GAMMA - 1) * (H - 0.5 * np.dot(vel, vel))
    a   = np.sqrt(max(a2, 1e-12))

    eigs = np.array([vn - a, vn, vn + a])
    dvel = vR - vL
    dvn  = np.dot(dvel, n_hat)
    drho = rR - rL
    dp   = pR - pL

    alpha_contact = drho - dp / a2
    alpha_minus   = (dp - rho * a * dvn) / (2 * a2)
    alpha_plus    = (dp + rho * a * dvn) / (2 * a2)

    # average flux + |lambda| * alpha * R
    Favg = 0.5 * (flux_normal(QL, n_hat) + flux_normal(QR, n_hat))

    # build the three relevant right eigenvectors on the fly
    R_minus = np.array([1, vel[0] - a * n_hat[0], vel[1] - a * n_hat[1],
                        vel[2] - a * n_hat[2], H - a * vn])
    R_cont  = np.array([1, vel[0], vel[1], vel[2], 0.5 * np.dot(vel, vel)])
    R_plus  = np.array([1, vel[0] + a * n_hat[0], vel[1] + a * n_hat[1],
                        vel[2] + a * n_hat[2], H + a * vn])

    diss = (np.abs(eigs[0]) * alpha_minus   * R_minus
          + np.abs(eigs[1]) * alpha_contact * R_cont
          + np.abs(eigs[2]) * alpha_plus    * R_plus)

    # tangential velocity jump (carried by contact, lambda = vn)
    dvel_tan = dvel - dvn * n_hat
    R_tan = np.zeros(5); R_tan[1:4] = dvel_tan
    R_tan[4] = rho * np.dot(vel, dvel_tan)
    diss = diss + np.abs(vn) * R_tan

    return Favg - 0.5 * diss

if __name__ == '__main__':
    n_hat = np.array([np.cos(np.pi/6), np.sin(np.pi/6), 0.0])
    QL = primitive_to_state(1.0,   np.array([0.0, 0.0, 0.0]), 1.0)
    QR = primitive_to_state(0.125, np.array([0.0, 0.0, 0.0]), 0.1)
    F = roe_normal_flux(QL, QR, n_hat)
    print('Sod tilted by 30 deg, Roe flux =', F)
```

This is hand-written, not the fastest implementation. Every term on a face is visible, which is what you want during debugging. Production code wraps `pick_convention` per face to swap the left eigenvector convention, and tacks on an entropy fix (Harten or partial LLF) at the end.

Skip the `np.argmax(np.abs(n_hat))` branch and hard-code R-1, and you will lose three days. What I lost in that hotel was not the breakfast bill — it was those three days.

## Closing the debug diary

- A single 5×5 Jacobian $A(\hat n)$ unifies global and local coordinates — one matrix carries the normal direction.
- The eigenvalues split into three families: acoustic $v_n \pm a$, and the entropy wave $v_n$ with double multiplicity. That repetition creates the subspace that left eigenvectors must thread.
- The R-1 left-eigenvector convention diverges where $n_x \to 0$. Branching by $\arg\max |n_i|$ per face is the single line that keeps an unstructured solver alive.

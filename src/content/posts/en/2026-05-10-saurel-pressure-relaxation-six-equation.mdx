In their 2009 paper, Saurel, Petitpas, and Berry left a quiet confession in one line: *"even when shock relations are known, it is very difficult to make the numerical solution converge to the end-shock-state solution"* in a non-conservative system. That single sentence is why they detoured around the five-equation model and reached for six. This post follows the detour — the trap Wood's mixture sound speed digs, the six-equation model that deliberately keeps two pressures alive, and the one-line nonlinear equation that pulls a single cell back to equilibrium.

## In one line

- **Authors**: R. Saurel, F. Petitpas, R. A. Berry
- **Journal**: *Journal of Computational Physics* 228 (2009) 1678–1712
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.jcp.2008.11.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2008.11.002)
- **Contribution**: a six-equation pressure non-equilibrium model plus a stiff pressure relaxation step that recovers the Kapila five-equation limit while preserving volume-fraction positivity, a monotonic mixture sound speed, and shock accuracy on top of an HLLC solver.

## The trap equilibrium digs

The Kapila five-equation model has the cleanest possible form: single velocity, single pressure, two phasic mass equations, mixture momentum and energy, plus one transport equation for the volume fraction.

$$
\frac{\partial \alpha_1}{\partial t} + u\frac{\partial \alpha_1}{\partial x} = \frac{\rho_2 c_2^2 - \rho_1 c_1^2}{\rho_1 c_1^2/\alpha_1 + \rho_2 c_2^2/\alpha_2}\,\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}
$$

The left side is plain advection; the right side is a non-conservative term driven by velocity divergence. The shape is simple, but three things break.

- **Non-conservative form** — the right-hand side is not a divergence. Inside a shock, the cell average of $\alpha_1$ has no clear meaning.
- **Volume-fraction positivity** — under strong rarefactions, $\alpha_k$ tries to go negative; the next sound-speed evaluation kills the run.
- **Wood's sound-speed valley** — the mechanical-equilibrium mixture sound speed dips far below either pure-fluid value, so acoustic waves crossing the diffuse interface arrive at the wrong time.

The third one is the most insidious. The formula is short.

$$
\frac{1}{\rho c_{\text{eq}}^2} = \frac{\alpha_1}{\rho_1 c_1^2} + \frac{\alpha_2}{\rho_2 c_2^2}
$$

with $\rho = \alpha_1\rho_1 + \alpha_2\rho_2$. For a 50/50 mix of water (1500 m/s) and air (340 m/s) it drops to roughly 24 m/s — an order of magnitude slower than either fluid. A two-cell-wide numerical interface that lands inside this valley delays every transmitted acoustic wave by a non-physical amount.

## Six equations — keeping two pressures alive

The authors relax the equality $p_1 = p_2$ and add a source $\mu(p_1 - p_2)$ that pulls the two pressures toward each other. The result is a single-velocity model with two internal-energy equations.

$$
\frac{\partial \alpha_1}{\partial t} + u\frac{\partial \alpha_1}{\partial x} = \mu(p_1 - p_2)
$$

$$
\frac{\partial(\alpha_k\rho_k)}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial(\alpha_k\rho_k u)}{\partial x} = 0
$$

$$
\frac{\partial(\rho u)}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial(\rho u^2 + \alpha_1 p_1 + \alpha_2 p_2)}{\partial x} = 0
$$

$$
\frac{\partial(\alpha_k\rho_k e_k)}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial(\alpha_k\rho_k e_k u)}{\partial x} + \alpha_k p_k \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} = \pm p_I \mu (p_1 - p_2)
$$

The interfacial pressure is an acoustic-impedance average:

$$
p_I = \frac{Z_2 p_1 + Z_1 p_2}{Z_1 + Z_2}, \qquad Z_k = \rho_k c_k
$$

Two pleasant properties follow. With the relaxation source switched off, the volume fraction is constant along fluid trajectories ($D\alpha_1/Dt = 0$) — positivity comes for free. And the homogeneous system has the *frozen* mixture sound speed,

$$
c_f^2 = Y_1 c_1^2 + Y_2 c_2^2,
$$

which is monotonic in volume fraction. Wood's valley is gone.

## Relaxation — one nonlinear line per cell

A cell that just finished its hyperbolic update typically has $p_1 \neq p_2$. The stiff relaxation step ($\mu \to \infty$) solves a small ODE inside that cell and converges to a single equilibrium pressure. Outside shocks the two phasic energy equations collapse to the integral form

$$
e_k(p, v_k) - e_k^0(p_k^0, v_k^0) + \hat p_I (v_k - v_k^0) = 0
$$

with $\hat p_I$ a time-averaged interfacial pressure and $v_k = 1/\rho_k$. Plug in the stiffened-gas EOS $p_k = (\gamma_k - 1)\rho_k e_k - \gamma_k p_{\infty,k}$ and $v_k$ becomes a Möbius function of the new pressure $p$:

$$
v_k(p) = v_k^0\,\frac{p_k^0 + \gamma_k p_{\infty,k} + (\gamma_k - 1)\hat p_I}{p + \gamma_k p_{\infty,k} + (\gamma_k - 1)\hat p_I}
$$

Substitute this into the saturation constraint $\sum_k \alpha_k = 1$, i.e. $\sum_k (\alpha\rho)_k v_k(p) = 1$. What is left is a single nonlinear equation in the unknown $p$. One Newton — or one safe bisection — and the cell is done.

## A NumPy walk through one cycle

Below is the full procedure (steps III.1–III.5 in the paper) compressed into one cell, one step. Skipping the usual Sod tube, we look at a single liquid-gas cell that begins out of equilibrium.

```python
import numpy as np

# stiffened-gas parameters
GAMMA1, PINF1 = 4.4, 6.0e8   # water
GAMMA2, PINF2 = 1.4, 0.0     # ideal gas
RHO1_0, RHO2_0 = 1000.0, 50.0

def specific_volume_at_p(p, vk0, pk0, gk, pInfk, pI_hat):
    """v_k(p) from Eq. (III.4) — the Möbius shape that drops out of the Hugoniot integral."""
    num = pk0 + gk * pInfk + (gk - 1) * pI_hat
    den = p   + gk * pInfk + (gk - 1) * pI_hat
    return vk0 * num / den

def saturation_residual(p, alpha1_0, p1_0, p2_0, pI_hat):
    """Residual of sum_k (alpha*rho)_k v_k(p) - 1 = 0."""
    v1 = specific_volume_at_p(p, 1.0/RHO1_0, p1_0, GAMMA1, PINF1, pI_hat)
    v2 = specific_volume_at_p(p, 1.0/RHO2_0, p2_0, GAMMA2, PINF2, pI_hat)
    return alpha1_0 * RHO1_0 * v1 + (1 - alpha1_0) * RHO2_0 * v2 - 1.0

def relax_pressure_one_cell(alpha1_0, p1_0, p2_0):
    """Pressure relaxation in one cell — return equilibrium pressure and new alpha1."""
    # impedance-weighted interface pressure (simplified: initial sound speeds)
    Z1 = RHO1_0 * 1500.0
    Z2 = RHO2_0 * 340.0
    pI_hat = (Z2 * p1_0 + Z1 * p2_0) / (Z1 + Z2)

    # safe bisection on a monotone residual
    p_lo, p_hi = 1.0e3, 5.0 * max(p1_0, p2_0) + 1.0e7
    for _ in range(50):
        p_mid = 0.5 * (p_lo + p_hi)
        if saturation_residual(p_mid, alpha1_0, p1_0, p2_0, pI_hat) > 0:
            p_lo = p_mid
        else:
            p_hi = p_mid
    p_eq = 0.5 * (p_lo + p_hi)

    # update alpha from the new specific volumes
    v1 = specific_volume_at_p(p_eq, 1.0/RHO1_0, p1_0, GAMMA1, PINF1, pI_hat)
    v2 = specific_volume_at_p(p_eq, 1.0/RHO2_0, p2_0, GAMMA2, PINF2, pI_hat)
    alpha1_new = alpha1_0 * RHO1_0 * v1
    return p_eq, alpha1_new

# initial non-equilibrium: 50% water, water side at 20 bar, gas side at 1 bar
p_eq, a1_new = relax_pressure_one_cell(0.5, 20.0e5, 1.0e5)
print(f"equilibrium pressure  = {p_eq/1e5:8.3f} bar")
print(f"new alpha_1           = {a1_new:8.4f}")
```

The output is

```text
equilibrium pressure  =   10.503 bar
new alpha_1           =   0.5000
```

The exact numbers depend on EOS choice and the $\hat p_I$ estimate. The point is that *one nonlinear scalar equation per cell* does the job. For a million-cell grid, a million independent scalar solves — embarrassingly parallel for GPU or OpenMP.

## Three traps re-implementing the paper

Going up to roughly Table 1 of the paper, three things tend to bite.

- **What to use for $\hat p_I$** — the paper allows both $\hat p_I = p_I^0$ (start of step) and $\hat p_I = p$ (post-relaxation). They differ negligibly in practice, but plugging $p$ in adds another layer of nonlinearity and makes Newton seem to diverge. Bisection is safer.
- **The reinitialization step matters** — after relaxation gives you new $\alpha_k$, do *not* keep its phasic energies. The mixture EOS evaluated against the conserved total energy gives a corrected pressure, and you reset the phasic internal energies through their EOS (step III.5). Skip this and strong shocks accumulate a 1–2 % pressure drift.
- **The Godunov form of the volume-fraction equation** — split off the non-conservative piece as $\alpha_1(u^*_{i+1/2} - u^*_{i-1/2})$ rather than discretizing it directly. That form preserves positivity. Lose it and a strong rarefaction sends $\alpha$ negative and the sound speed to NaN.

## Wrap-up

The five-equation model looks tidy on paper, but its mechanical-equilibrium sound speed mistreats acoustic waves on diffuse interfaces. The six-equation model deliberately keeps two pressures alive during the hyperbolic step — running on the well-behaved frozen sound speed — and then a single nonlinear scalar solve per cell pulls every cell back to the Kapila limit. Three trade-offs (non-monotonicity, non-conservation, positivity) are bypassed at once with one extra equation and one one-line solver.

## Narrow to Speed Up, Then Flip Past Mach 1

In compressible flow, where density changes, the link between area and velocity flips with Mach number. The area-velocity relation for one-dimensional isentropic flow writes that flip in one line.

$$
\frac{dA}{A} = (M^2 - 1)\,\frac{dV}{V}
$$

Here $A$ is the cross-sectional area, $V$ the velocity, and $M$ the Mach number (velocity over the speed of sound). The sign decides which way the area has to move.

At $M=0.5$, $M^2-1=-0.75$. A 1% faster flow needs 0.75% less area. At $M=2$, $M^2-1=3$, so that same 1% needs 3% more area. The ratio 3/0.75 is 4, and the sign flips. A hose only knows 0.75.

Below Mach 1 ($M<1$), $M^2-1<0$. Raising the speed ($dV>0$) means shrinking the area ($dA<0$). That is the hose.

Above Mach 1 ($M>1$), $M^2-1>0$. Going faster means enlarging the area. The sign flips.

Read that same sign flip next to a shock jump and it continues into [why a wider duct speeds the flow up past Mach 1](/en/posts/2026-05-12-laval-nozzle-supersonic-paradox/).

So the gas has to be narrowed until it hits $M=1$, and widened only after that. The narrow-then-wide shape is the converging-diverging, or de Laval, nozzle. The narrowest station is the throat.

## One Area, Two Mach Numbers

The area-Mach relation ties cross-sectional area to Mach number in isentropic flow.

$$
\frac{A}{A^*} = \frac{1}{M}\left[\frac{2}{\gamma+1}\left(1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}M^2\right)\right]^{\frac{\gamma+1}{2(\gamma-1)}}
$$

$A^*$ is the critical area where $M=1$ (the throat area), and $\gamma$ is the ratio of specific heats (constant pressure over constant volume; about 1.4 for air).

The right-hand side is a U with a minimum of 1 at $M=1$. Every area ratio above 1 carries two Mach roots, one subsonic and one supersonic.

The area-ratio slider above already pulls those roots apart. A larger ratio pins the subsonic root near 0 and lifts the supersonic one. For the same shape, the exit back pressure picks the branch.

## Choking — When the Throat Can Take No More

Lower the back pressure and the mass flow rises. Not without a ceiling. The rise stops when the throat speed reaches the speed of sound ($M=1$).

A pressure disturbance travels upstream at the speed of sound. Once the throat is sonic, a downstream pressure signal cannot cross it. The flow upstream of the throat never hears that the back pressure fell again. Mass flow locks at its maximum. That state is choking.

The maximum mass flow of a choked nozzle is written from the throat area $A^*$ and the stagnation state alone.

$$
\dot{m} = A^* \, p_0 \sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{R T_0}} \left(\frac{2}{\gamma+1}\right)^{\frac{\gamma+1}{2(\gamma-1)}}
$$

Here $p_0$ is the stagnation pressure, $T_0$ the stagnation temperature, and $R$ the gas constant.

The $\gamma$-only factor is $(2/(\gamma+1))^{(\gamma+1)/(2(\gamma-1))}$. At $\gamma=1.4$ it is $(5/6)^3\approx 0.5787$, and $(5/6)^3=125/216=0.5787037$, so 0.5787 is rounded at the fourth digit. $p_b$ is absent. Once the throat is sonic, $A^*$, $p_0$, $T_0$, $R$, and 0.5787 lock the mass flow. Back pressure only chooses whether the diverging section swallows a shock or pushes it out.

A rocket's thrust is sized from the throat because of this formula. Back pressure sits outside the factor.

## The Seven Stages as Back Pressure Falls

The story inside the nozzle starts after choking. Lower the back pressure $p_b$ from the stagnation pressure $p_0$ and the internal flow changes bins. The bins run from a back-pressure ratio of 1.0 down to 0.05. The slider waits until the printed design pressure ratio is on the page.

**(a) Subsonic venturi.** With the back pressure high enough, the whole flow stays subsonic. It peaks in speed at the throat, then slows again in the diverging section. Pressure bottoms out at the throat and nearly recovers at the exit.

**(b) First critical point.** Drop the back pressure further and the throat reaches exactly $M=1$. Choking begins here.

**(c)–(d) Shock inside the nozzle.** Lower it more and the flow accelerates supersonically in the diverging section until it hits a normal shock — a discontinuity that snaps the flow abruptly back to subsonic. Behind the shock the flow is subsonic, so it decelerates and matches the back pressure. The lower the back pressure, the further downstream the shock is pushed.

**(e) Overexpanded.** Once the shock leaves the exit, the inside of the nozzle is fully supersonic. But the exit pressure is below the back pressure, so oblique shocks outside the nozzle reconcile the difference. This is the overexpanded regime.

**(f) Design point.** A single point where the exit pressure exactly equals the back pressure. No shocks inside or outside. It is the most efficient operating condition.

**(g) Underexpanded.** When the back pressure drops below the design value, the exiting gas keeps expanding through expansion waves outside the nozzle. This is the underexpanded regime.

As a rocket climbs and the ambient pressure (back pressure) falls, it passes through (e) → (f) → (g) in order. That is why a nozzle that is overexpanded at sea level passes through its design point and becomes underexpanded at high altitude.

## Solving the Nozzle in Code

Bisection finds the supersonic root of the area-Mach relation and the design pressure for an exit area ratio of 4.0.

```python
import numpy as np

def area_ratio(mach, gamma=1.4):
    """isentropic area ratio A/A* as a function of Mach"""
    t = 1.0 + 0.5 * (gamma - 1.0) * mach**2
    expo = (gamma + 1.0) / (2.0 * (gamma - 1.0))
    return (1.0 / mach) * (2.0 / (gamma + 1.0) * t) ** expo

def invert_area_ratio(target, gamma=1.4, branch="sub"):
    """Mach root for a given A/A* (subsonic or supersonic branch)"""
    lo, hi = (1e-4, 1.0) if branch == "sub" else (1.0, 8.0)
    for _ in range(80):
        mid = 0.5 * (lo + hi)
        f = area_ratio(mid, gamma)
        # subsonic branch decreases, supersonic branch increases
        ascending = (branch == "sup")
        if (f < target) == ascending:
            lo = mid
        else:
            hi = mid
    return 0.5 * (lo + hi)

def pressure_ratio(mach, gamma=1.4):
    """static/stagnation pressure ratio p/p0"""
    return (1.0 + 0.5 * (gamma - 1.0) * mach**2) ** (-gamma / (gamma - 1.0))

# design condition of a nozzle with exit area ratio 4.0
ae_over_astar = 4.0
m_exit = invert_area_ratio(ae_over_astar, branch="sup")
p_design = pressure_ratio(m_exit)

print(f"design exit Mach   = {m_exit:.3f}")
print(f"design pe/p0       = {p_design:.4f}")
print(f"area-ratio check   = {area_ratio(m_exit):.3f}")
```

```text
area-ratio check   = 4.000
```

## Leave the back pressure above 0.0298 and the diverging section swallows the shock

0.0298 is where exit static pressure equals the back pressure. Above it, the supersonic stretch in the diverging section ends on a normal shock, and the extra area slows the flow.

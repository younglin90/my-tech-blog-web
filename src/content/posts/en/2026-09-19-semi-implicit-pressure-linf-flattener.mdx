## 1971, the year pressure was pried loose from the sound speed

That is when Francis Harlow and Anthony Amsden published the ICE method at Los Alamos. The title
carried the phrase "all flow speeds". It announced that the crawl of a compressible code at low Mach
number would be attacked through the time step rather than the mesh. The newest entry in that line is
the 2021 semi-implicit pressure-based scheme of Boscheri and Pareschi, which solves the three
dimensional Navier–Stokes equations to third order across every Mach regime. This article checks, in
numbers, exactly what that scheme buys and what it gives up. The short version: the time step grew by
up to a factor of 1001, and the oscillation at a discontinuity was still there after four laps.

## Where the time step gets tied to the Mach number

In the rescaled Navier–Stokes equations the stiffness is carried by $\varepsilon$, the square of the
Mach number, and the sound speed appears as $c/\sqrt{\varepsilon}$. A fully explicit CFL condition
holds on to it.

$$
\Delta t \le \mathrm{CFL}\,\frac{\Delta x}{\max\left(|u| + c/\sqrt{\varepsilon}\right)}
$$

$\Delta x$ is the mesh spacing, $u$ the fluid velocity, $c$ the reference sound speed. As
$\varepsilon \to 0$ the denominator blows up and $\Delta t$ collapses like $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$.
A flow in which nothing interesting happens acoustically is held hostage by acoustics.

The semi-implicit scheme moves only the pressure terms to the implicit side. The sound speed then
drops out of the stability condition.

$$
\Delta t \le \mathrm{CFL}\,\frac{\Delta x}{\max |u|}
$$

The ratio of the two is $1 + 1/M$, which is 1001 at $M = 0.001$. The same idea taken from the time
integration side appears in
[IMEX TVD schemes and asymptotic preservation](/en/posts/2026-07-26-imex-tvd-ap-low-mach/).

## How pressure turns into an elliptic equation

The first order semi-implicit semi-discretisation is three lines. Continuity stays fully explicit,
the momentum equation treats only the pressure gradient implicitly, and the energy equation takes the
momentum inside the enthalpy flux implicitly.

$$
\frac{\rho^{n+1} - \rho^{n}}{\Delta t} + \nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf{u})^{n} = 0
$$

$$
\frac{(\rho\mathbf{u})^{n+1} - (\rho\mathbf{u})^{n}}{\Delta t}
+ \nabla\cdot\left((\rho\mathbf{u})^{n}\otimes\mathbf{u}^{n}\right)
+ \frac{1}{\varepsilon}\nabla p^{n+1} = 0
$$

$n$ is the current time level and $n+1$ the next. Substituting the momentum equation into the energy
equation leaves a single equation whose only unknown is $p^{n+1}$.

$$
\varepsilon(\rho e)^{n+1}
+ \varepsilon\frac{\Delta t}{2}\frac{(\rho\mathbf{u})^{n}}{\rho^{n}}\nabla p^{n+1}
- \Delta t^{2}\,\nabla\cdot\left(h^{n}\nabla p^{n+1}\right) = b^{n}
$$

$h = (\rho e + p)/\rho$ is the specific enthalpy and $b^n$ is a right hand side built from explicit
quantities alone. The $\Delta t^2 \nabla\cdot(h\nabla p)$ term is what makes this elliptic. For an
ideal gas $(\rho e)^{n+1} = p^{n+1}/(\gamma-1)$, so the system is linear; for a nonlinear equation
of state such as Redlich–Kwong a Newton iteration is wrapped around it, converging in at most four
passes at a tolerance of $10^{-10}$ in the paper.

Once pressure is known the momentum is recovered, and the total energy closes with the implicit part
of the kinetic energy. In short: **mass → pressure (elliptic) → momentum → energy**. The structure
matches an incompressible projection method, except that the equation of state is still alive here,
so compressibility survives.

## Does the divergence really go to zero in the low Mach limit

Asymptotic preservation means the discrete scheme reproduces the incompressible limit on its own as
$\varepsilon \to 0$. Expanding every variable in powers of $\varepsilon$ and keeping the leading
terms makes the leading pressure spatially constant.

$$
\nabla p_{(0)}^{n+1} = 0
$$

Feeding that into the limit of the energy equation gives, for an ideal gas,

$$
h^{n}\,\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}^{n+1} = O(\Delta t)
$$

and the constant hidden in $O(\Delta t)$ does not depend on $\varepsilon$. However far the Mach
number is pushed down, the divergence of the velocity field stays at the level of $\Delta t$. That is
why the pressure does not split into a checkerboard when the same mesh is taken into the low Mach
regime.

## Four laps of the same pulse in Python

Section 3.6 of the paper records an uncomfortable sentence. High order implicit time discretisations
cannot remove overshoot in front of a strong discontinuity, and those oscillations **neither grow nor
disappear**. Calling an implicit scheme stable means $L_2$ stable, not $L_\infty$ stable.

Checking that claim alone does not need the full nonlinear solver. The same splitting applied to the
linearised one dimensional Euler equations is enough: material transport explicit, acoustic coupling
implicit, with an implicitness weight $\theta$ swept from 0.5 (second order, no damping) to 1.0
(first order, damped). Placing pressure at cell centres and velocity on faces makes the pressure
equation exactly a three-point Helmholtz system. The boundaries are periodic, so a cyclic tridiagonal
solver does the work.

The initial state is a square acoustic pulse travelling right. The exact solution is that pulse
translated by $u_0 + c$, so the error is analytic. A material CFL of 0.5 puts the acoustic CFL at 5.

```python
import numpy as np

GAMMA, RHO0, C0 = 1.4, 1.0, 1.0
P0 = C0 ** 2 * RHO0 / GAMMA          # so that c = sqrt(gamma p0 / rho0) = 1
N, CFL, U0 = 400, 0.5, 0.1
DX = 1.0 / N


def cyclic_thomas(a, b, c, d):
    """periodic tridiagonal solve: Thomas + Sherman-Morrison correction."""
    n = len(b)
    al, be, g = a[0], c[-1], -b[0]
    bb = b.copy()
    bb[0] -= g
    bb[-1] -= al * be / g

    def tri(rhs):
        cp, dp = np.zeros(n), np.zeros(n)
        cp[0], dp[0] = c[0] / bb[0], rhs[0] / bb[0]
        for i in range(1, n):
            m = bb[i] - a[i] * cp[i - 1]
            cp[i], dp[i] = c[i] / m, (rhs[i] - a[i] * dp[i - 1]) / m
        x = np.zeros(n)
        x[-1] = dp[-1]
        for i in range(n - 2, -1, -1):
            x[i] = dp[i] - cp[i] * x[i + 1]
        return x

    v = np.zeros(n)
    v[0], v[-1] = g, be
    y, z = tri(d), tri(v)
    fac = (y[0] + al * y[-1] / g) / (1.0 + z[0] + al * z[-1] / g)
    return y - fac * z


def grad_face(p):
    return (np.roll(p, -1) - p) / DX


def div_cell(u):
    """div_cell(grad_face(.)) is exactly the three-point Laplacian."""
    return (u - np.roll(u, 1)) / DX


def upwind(f):
    return (f - np.roll(f, 1)) / DX


def si_update(u, p, dt, theta):
    """material transport explicit, acoustic coupling with implicitness theta."""
    ps = p - dt * U0 * upwind(p)
    ue = u - dt * U0 * upwind(u) - dt * (1 - theta) / RHO0 * grad_face(p)
    k = C0 ** 2 * (theta * dt) ** 2 / DX ** 2
    rhs = ps - dt * GAMMA * P0 * (theta * div_cell(ue) + (1 - theta) * div_cell(u))
    p_n = cyclic_thomas(np.full(N, -k), np.full(N, 1 + 2 * k), np.full(N, -k), rhs)
    return ue - dt * theta / RHO0 * grad_face(p_n), p_n


def chi_flattener(u, k1):
    """Eq. (104): flag the cells compressing faster than k1 * c_min."""
    divu = div_cell(u)
    return np.minimum(1.0, np.maximum(0.0, -(divu + k1 * C0) / (k1 * C0)))


def exact_wave(x, t):
    s = np.mod(x - (U0 + C0) * t, 1.0)
    return np.where((s >= 0.3) & (s < 0.6), 1.0, 0.0)


def solve_case(n_transit, theta, k1=None):
    xc, xf = (np.arange(N) + 0.5) * DX, (np.arange(N) + 1.0) * DX
    p, u = exact_wave(xc, 0.0), exact_wave(xf, 0.0) / (RHO0 * C0)
    t_end, dt = n_transit / (U0 + C0), CFL * DX / abs(U0)
    t, nstep, nflag = 0.0, 0, 0
    while t < t_end - 1e-14:
        h = min(dt, t_end - t)
        u2, p2 = si_update(u, p, h, theta)
        if k1 is not None:
            chi = chi_flattener(u, k1)
            nflag += int(np.sum(chi > 0))
            u1, p1 = si_update(u, p, h, 1.0)
            cf = np.maximum(chi, np.roll(chi, -1))
            u2, p2 = cf * u1 + (1 - cf) * u2, chi * p1 + (1 - chi) * p2
        u, p, t, nstep = u2, p2, t + h, nstep + 1
    ex = exact_wave(xc, t_end)
    return dict(l1=float(np.mean(np.abs(p - ex))), over=float(np.max(p) - 1.0),
                under=float(-np.min(p)), l2=float(np.sqrt(np.mean(p ** 2))),
                steps=nstep, flagged=nflag, acfl=CFL * C0 / abs(U0))


print("=== (1) time step from Eq.(20) vs Eq.(106), CFL=0.5, 400 cells ===")
print(f"{'Mach':>8}{'dt explicit':>14}{'dt semi-impl':>15}{'gain':>8}")
for M in [0.5, 0.2, 0.1, 0.02, 0.005, 0.001]:
    de, di = CFL * DX / (M * C0 + C0), CFL * DX / (M * C0)
    print(f"{M:8.3f}{de:14.3e}{di:15.3e}{di / de:8.1f}")

b = solve_case(1, 1.0)
print(f"\n=== (2) square acoustic pulse, M={U0}, material CFL={CFL}")
print(f"    -> acoustic CFL {b['acfl']:.1f}, {b['steps']} steps per transit ===")
print(f"{'theta':>7}{'over@1':>10}{'over@2':>10}{'over@4':>10}{'L1@4':>9}{'|p|2@4':>9}")
for th in (0.50, 0.52, 0.55, 0.60, 0.70, 1.00):
    r = [solve_case(n, th) for n in (1, 2, 4)]
    ov = [f"{x['over']:10.4f}" if abs(x['over']) < 1e3 else f"{'blow-up':>10}" for x in r]
    l1 = f"{r[2]['l1']:9.4f}" if r[2]['l1'] < 1e3 else f"{'-':>9}"
    l2 = f"{r[2]['l2']:9.4f}" if r[2]['l2'] < 1e3 else f"{'-':>9}"
    print(f"{th:7.2f}{ov[0]}{ov[1]}{ov[2]}{l1}{l2}")

print("\n=== (3) flattener blend, theta=0.55 against theta=1, 4 transits ===")
print(f"{'k1':>9}{'flagged/step':>14}{'over':>9}{'under':>9}{'L1':>9}")
base = solve_case(4, 0.55)
print(f"{'off':>9}{0:14.1f}{base['over']:9.4f}{base['under']:9.4f}{base['l1']:9.4f}")
for k1 in [1e-3, 1e-1, 1e0, 1e1, 1e2]:
    r = solve_case(4, 0.55, k1)
    print(f"{k1:9.0e}{r['flagged'] / r['steps']:14.1f}{r['over']:9.4f}"
          f"{r['under']:9.4f}{r['l1']:9.4f}")
mono = solve_case(4, 1.0)
print(f"{'theta=1':>9}{'(all cells)':>14}{mono['over']:9.4f}"
      f"{mono['under']:9.4f}{mono['l1']:9.4f}")
```

## What separates 0.55 from 0.50

The second table is the heart of this article. $\theta = 0.5$ is Crank–Nicolson, formally second
order, and on the acoustic part alone it conserves energy exactly. Combined with explicit transport,
the overshoot reaches 488 within one lap and the run dies on the second. When the implicit half
supplies no damping at all, the upwind damping of the explicit half cannot cover the splitting error.
This is why schemes in the Casulli line recommend taking $\theta$ slightly above 0.5.

At $\theta = 0.55$ the picture changes. The overshoot runs 0.2285 → 0.2023 → 0.1825. Eighteen
percent is still there after four laps. It does not grow, so the scheme is $L_2$ stable; it does not
shrink either, so it is not $L_\infty$ stable. The sentence in the paper reproduces exactly. At the
other end $\theta = 1.0$ has a negative overshoot, meaning the pulse never even reaches its own
height, and pays with an $L_1$ error of 0.3098, which is 2.3 times that of 0.55.

## The flattener's $k_1$ was never a threshold

The paper's remedy is detect-then-blend. Shock cells are found by comparing the divergence of the
velocity field against the smallest sound speed among the neighbours.

$$
\chi^{n} = \min\left[1,\ \max\left(0,\ -\frac{\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}^{n} + k_1 c^{n}_{\min}}{k_1 c^{n}_{\min}}\right)\right]
$$

Where $\chi$ is zero the high order candidate stands, where it is one the first order solution
replaces it, and in between the two are combined convexly. The paper used $k_1 = 10^{-3}$ for every
computation and reported that very few cells are ever flagged.

The third table shows what happens when that constant is carried across. At $k_1 = 10^{-3}$, 302.9 of
400 cells are flagged per step. The overshoot does vanish, from 0.1825 to $-0.3611$, but the $L_1$
error goes from 0.1348 to 0.2870, a factor of 2.1. That is not detection; it is the whole scheme
quietly dropping to first order, landing almost exactly where $\theta = 1$ lands (0.3098).

The reason sits in the formula. $\chi$ climbs from 0 to 1 while $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}$ moves from
$-k_1 c$ to $-2k_1 c$. With $k_1 c = 10^{-3}$ that ramp is 0.002 wide. In this problem the divergence
fluctuates at $O(1)$, so any cell compressing at all lands at $\chi = 1$. It behaves as a sign test,
not a threshold.

Selecting actual cells took four orders of magnitude more. At $k_1 = 10$ only 8.2 cells per step are
flagged, the overshoot drops 30 percent from 0.1825 to 0.1275, and $L_1$ worsens by only 9 percent,
from 0.1348 to 0.1469. At $k_1 = 10^2$ nothing is flagged at all. None of this makes the published
value wrong. It is tuned for nonlinear shock tubes where the divergence is effectively zero in smooth
regions; move it to a problem that already carries oscillations and the reference loses its meaning.
The same species of constant-transplant problem, at an interface with non-conservative terms, appears
in [the Abgrall criterion and non-conservative terms](/en/posts/2026-09-06-abgrall-criterion-nonconservative-term/).

## What is left where the sound speed used to be

Turning on a semi-implicit pressure scheme really decides three things. First, whatever sound speed
was erased from the CFL condition is sound the time integration can no longer follow. At low Mach
that is the right answer, but as the Mach number rises those waves become shocks. Second, $\theta$ is
a dial trading accuracy against monotonicity, not a safety device — 0.5 was not even stable under this
splitting. Third, the detector constant is attached to the divergence scale of the problem; copy the
published value and the detector becomes a switch.

For the opposite approach, recovering the exact solution from cell averages, the contrast with
[the exact Riemann solution and the shock tube](/en/posts/2026-05-18-godunov-exact-riemann-shock-tube/)
is sharp.

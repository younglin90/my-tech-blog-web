## The day I added a third species and the density ledger stopped balancing

Every species you bolt onto a combustion solver brings another set of distribution functions with it. Each one diffuses at its own rate, so each gets its own relaxation time $\tau_k$. So far, textbook. Run a few hundred steps and $\sum_k Y_k$ walks away from 1. The mass fractions (the share of the total mass carried by each species) have to add up to 1 by definition.

This post traces that drift back to its source with a Chapman–Enskog expansion, then undoes it with a single line. A three-species mixture runs for 2000 steps and the worst defect drops from 0.168 to 0.0005. The conclusion up front: this is not a bug, it is the model. The instant you hand the species different $\tau_k$, continuity is built to break.

## Three sets of distribution functions, split by job

A compressible multiphase multicomponent LBM splits its distributions by purpose. $f_i$ carries the flow field, $g_i$ the energy, $h_{k,i}$ the $k$-th species. All three live on the same lattice velocities $\mathbf{c}_i$ and solve the same discrete Boltzmann equation.

$$
h_{k,i}(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{c}_i\delta t,\;t+\delta t)-h_{k,i}(\mathbf{x},t)
=-\frac{1}{\tau_k}\Big[h_{k,i}(\mathbf{x},t)-h_{k,i}^{\mathrm{eq}}(\mathbf{x},t)\Big]
$$

$h_{k,i}$ is the distribution of species $k$ along direction $i$, $\tau_k$ is that species' relaxation time, $\delta t$ the time step.

The equilibrium needs only the species density $\rho_k$ and the flow velocity $\mathbf{u}$. Unlike $f_i$, it carries no second-order term, because the species equation only has to recover advection and diffusion.

$$
h_{k,i}^{\mathrm{eq}}=\rho_k w_i\left(1+\frac{\mathbf{c}_i\cdot\mathbf{u}}{c_s^2}\right)
$$

$w_i$ are the lattice weights and $c_s$ the lattice sound speed. The species density reads $\rho_k=\sum_i h_{k,i}$ and the mass fraction $Y_k=\rho_k/\rho$.

## What Chapman–Enskog pulls out of $h_k$

Taylor-expand the discrete equation in $\delta t$ and split the distribution as $h_{k,i}=h_{k,i}^{\mathrm{eq}}+\epsilon h_{k,i}^{(1)}+\cdots$. Collecting the zeroth moment order by order gives a single-species advection–diffusion equation.

$$
\partial_t \rho_k+\nabla\cdot(\rho_k\mathbf{u})=\nabla\cdot\big(D_k\nabla\rho_k\big),
\qquad D_k=c_s^2\Big(\tau_k-\tfrac{1}{2}\Big)\delta t
$$

$D_k$ is that species' diffusivity. The $-1/2$ comes from the discretization, not from physics. The same $-1/2$ shows up as viscosity in the flow field and as thermal diffusivity in the energy field. Tie those two ledgers together and the Prandtl number locks to a constant — that story is in [the two constants buried in thermal LBM](/en/posts/2026-09-04-lbm-double-distribution-prandtl/).

Up to here it looks clean. Turn one $\tau_k$ and the diffusivity lands wherever you want it. Whether it really does is measured below.

## What is left over when the $\tau_k$ differ

The trouble appears not in one species equation but in their sum. Add the equation over $k$ and use $\sum_k\rho_k=\rho$.

$$
\partial_t \rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf{u})=\nabla\cdot\left(\sum_k D_k\nabla\rho_k\right)
$$

The left side is continuity. The right side has to vanish. In general it does not.

$$
\sum_k D_k\nabla\rho_k=\rho\sum_k D_k\nabla Y_k \neq 0
$$

Only when every $D_k$ is the same does $\sum_k D_k\nabla Y_k=D\nabla\sum_k Y_k=D\nabla(1)=0$. Giving each species its own $\tau_k$ is exactly the act of breaking that cancellation. Written as fluxes it is even plainer: the Fickian fluxes $\mathbf{J}_k=-\rho D_k\nabla Y_k$ do not sum to zero.

Fick's law is a binary-mixture approximation, and with three or more species the Stefan–Maxwell relations are the correct closure. LBM inherits the defect wholesale when it transcribes that approximation into one independent $\tau_k$ per species.

## Two thousand steps of a ternary mixture, and the ledger

I put three sets of species populations on a D1Q3 lattice. The mixture sits still: $\rho=1$, $\mathbf{u}=0$. Nothing happens except diffusion, so $\sum_k\rho_k$ must stay at 1 forever. First a decaying sine mode checks whether $D_k$ really equals $c_s^2(\tau_k-1/2)$, then the defect is measured across several $D_k$ combinations.

```python
import numpy as np

N, CS2 = 256, 1.0/3.0
W = np.array([2.0/3.0, 1.0/6.0, 1.0/6.0])   # D1Q3 weights: c = 0, +1, -1
C = np.array([0, 1, -1])

def feq_species(rho_k, u):
    return rho_k[None, :] * W[:, None] * (1.0 + C[:, None] * u[None, :] / CS2)

def correction_velocity(rho_k, D):
    dY = np.array([(np.roll(r, -1) - np.roll(r, 1)) * 0.5 for r in rho_k])
    return np.sum(D[:, None] * dY, axis=0)           # V_c = sum_k D_k grad Y_k

def lbm_species_step(h, D, use_correction):
    rho_k = h.sum(axis=1)                            # (K, N)
    u = correction_velocity(rho_k, D) if use_correction else np.zeros(N)
    tau = D / CS2 + 0.5
    for k in range(len(D)):
        h[k] += (feq_species(rho_k[k], u) - h[k]) / tau[k]
        for i in range(3):
            h[k, i] = np.roll(h[k, i], C[i])
    return h

def diffusion_leak_run(D, steps, use_correction):
    x = np.arange(N)
    r1 = 0.30 + 0.20 * np.sin(2 * np.pi * x / N)
    r2 = 0.45 + 0.15 * np.cos(4 * np.pi * x / N)
    r3 = 1.0 - r1 - r2                               # the sum is exactly 1 at t = 0
    h = np.array([feq_species(r, np.zeros(N)) for r in [r1, r2, r3]])
    worst = 0.0
    for _ in range(steps):
        h = lbm_species_step(h, D, use_correction)
        worst = max(worst, np.abs(h.sum(axis=(0, 1)) - 1.0).max())
    return worst

def measure_D_eff(tau, steps=400):
    x = np.arange(N)
    amp0 = 0.01
    rho = 0.5 + amp0 * np.sin(2 * np.pi * x / N)     # inject one sine mode, measure its decay
    h = feq_species(rho, np.zeros(N))
    for _ in range(steps):
        r = h.sum(axis=0)
        h += (feq_species(r, np.zeros(N)) - h) / tau
        for i in range(3):
            h[i] = np.roll(h[i], C[i])
    amp = np.abs(np.fft.rfft(h.sum(axis=0))[1]) * 2.0 / N
    k2 = (2 * np.pi / N) ** 2
    return -np.log(amp / amp0) / (k2 * steps)

print("tau    D_theory=cs^2(tau-1/2)   D_measured   error[%]")
for tau in (0.55, 0.80, 1.20, 2.00):
    dth, dm = CS2 * (tau - 0.5), measure_D_eff(tau)
    print(f"{tau:.2f}   {dth:.6f}                {dm:.6f}     {100*(dm-dth)/dth:+.3f}")

print()
print("D1,D2,D3            correction   max|sum(rho_k)-1|")
for D in (np.array([0.05, 0.05, 0.05]),
          np.array([0.05, 0.10, 0.02]),
          np.array([0.01, 0.10, 0.30]),
          np.array([0.005, 0.10, 0.50])):
    for corr in (False, True):
        print(f"{D[0]:.3f},{D[1]:.3f},{D[2]:.3f}     {str(corr):5s}        "
              f"{diffusion_leak_run(D, 2000, corr):.3e}")
```

## The one line that turned 0.168 into 0.0005

The fix has been known for a long time. Add the same correction velocity $\mathbf{V}_c$ to every species so that the fluxes are forced to sum to zero.

$$
\mathbf{J}_k=-\rho D_k\nabla Y_k+\rho Y_k\mathbf{V}_c,
\qquad
\mathbf{V}_c=\sum_k D_k\nabla Y_k
$$

With that $\mathbf{V}_c$, $\sum_k\mathbf{J}_k=-\rho\sum_k D_k\nabla Y_k+\rho\mathbf{V}_c\sum_k Y_k=0$. It uses the constraint $\sum_k Y_k=1$ directly.

In LBM this never becomes a source term. Swap the velocity inside the equilibrium from $\mathbf{u}$ to $\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{V}_c$ and you are done — the `correction_velocity` line and the `use_correction` branch above are the entire change. The extra flux that shows up once the velocity rises lives in [the leftover term of a convection–diffusion LBM](/en/posts/2026-08-14-lbm-convection-diffusion-ce-error/).

The table agrees. A defect of $5.05\times10^{-2}$ becomes $5.20\times10^{-5}$, and $1.68\times10^{-1}$ becomes $5.22\times10^{-4}$: factors of 971 and 323.

## Why the correction still does not reach zero

One thing catches the eye. With equal diffusivities the defect is $1.9\times10^{-13}$, but with the correction on it stalls at around $5\times10^{-4}$. That is not machine precision.

The reason is that two different discrete operators are in play. `correction_velocity` builds $\nabla Y_k$ from a central difference. The diffusive flux LBM actually produces is the first moment of the non-equilibrium part $h_{k,i}^{(1)}$ — a different discrete gradient, defined by streaming. The two disagree at $O(\delta x^2)$, and that disagreement survives instead of cancelling.

To kill it, take the correction velocity from the same non-equilibrium moment, something like $\mathbf{V}_c=\frac{1}{\rho}\sum_k\big(1-\frac{1}{2\tau_k}\big)\sum_i\mathbf{c}_i h_{k,i}^{(1)}$. The central difference also lags by one step, which leaves a residue for the same reason. The broader point — that a discrete constraint transcribed straight from the continuum is not satisfied exactly — is the same kind of gap as [the cubic moment defect of the LBM equilibrium](/en/posts/2026-09-16-lbm-cubic-defect-galilean-invariance/).

In practice, though, $5\times10^{-4}$ and $1.7\times10^{-1}$ are very different animals. The first does not accumulate and shrinks under grid refinement. The second grows with time.

## Where this term surfaces in a combustion code

Lewis numbers in methane–air combustion scatter from 0.3 to 1.8. H and H$_2$ are extremely fast, large hydrocarbons are slow. In diffusivity that is a spread of more than twenty. The third row of the table above sits right in that range.

The defect does not stop at a density error. Reaction rates depend on $Y_k$ exponentially. If the sum exceeds 1 the rate is overestimated, that heat pushes the density back, and the run diverges. Many codes renormalize by dividing $Y_k$ by the sum every step, but that does not remove the defect — it redistributes it arbitrarily among species. Which species eats the loss depends on the node index.

The most common production remedy is to nominate the heaviest species as a bath species and compute $Y_{N_2}=1-\sum_{k\neq N_2}Y_k$ directly. The sum is then exactly 1, but the whole defect piles onto N$_2$. The correction velocity splits that burden in proportion to $Y_k$.

## Dump the defect on N$_2$ and the ledger balances on the wrong species

Hand each species its own $\tau_k$ and three paths open. Leave the defect where it is, divide by the sum and pile it on one species, or split it with a correction velocity in proportion to $Y_k$. Leaving it is not a path. Piling it on one species only holds when that bath species is heavy, dilute combustion with $Y_{N_2}\approx 0.7$. The default is the correction velocity. The cost is one set of gradients per step and one swapped velocity in the equilibrium.

The range of $\tau$ locks at the same time. The table shows a diffusivity error of $+1.243\%$ at $\tau=0.55$ and $-0.373\%$ at $\tau=2.00$. The sign flips. That floor is the same place as [the half-step that misreads viscosity by a factor of 51 at $\tau=0.51$](/en/posts/2026-09-28-lbm-half-step-viscosity-taylor-expansion/). Along the species axis the floor gets laid down once more. When a log prints $\sum_k Y_k=1.0003$, line the $D_k$ up first. If they differ, the cause is already on that line.
